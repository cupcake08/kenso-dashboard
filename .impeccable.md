# Kenso Dashboard — Design Context

## Users

**Shop owners and ops teams** opening the dashboard at the start of the day to understand what happened across their stores overnight. They manage multiple locations, need to act quickly on alerts, and don't have time to fight a confusing UI. Context: morning check-in, not deep-dive analysis. They scan, they act, they move on.

Secondary: Enterprise admins managing billing, device labels, and plans across an organization.

## Brand Personality

**Command center. Clean. No-nonsense.**

Three words: **Dense · Professional · Actionable**

The interface should feel like a well-engineered tool — not a marketing site, not a consumer app. Data is the hero. Every element earns its place.

## Aesthetic Direction

**Clean minimal** — light backgrounds, generous whitespace, refined typography. Not dark, not flashy. Light-mode primary.

Color is functional: emerald for positive/action, amber for warnings, red for errors, slate for structural elements. No decorative color.

Reference feel: Linear (clean precision), Vercel dashboard (professional restraint), Stripe dashboard (calm authority). NOT pastel, NOT playful, NOT decorative-gradient.

## Design Principles

1. **Density serves the operator** — More information per screen is a feature, not a problem. Compact layouts with clear hierarchy. Don't pad for aesthetics.

2. **Color is semantic, never decorative** — Emerald = positive/action. Amber = flag/warning. Red = error/offline. Blue = streaming/live. Every color choice carries meaning.

3. **Left-align, break the grid intentionally** — Don't center everything. Asymmetric layouts feel designed. Break the grid for emphasis, not by accident.

4. **No AI slop aesthetics** — No purple-blue gradients, no neon on dark, no glowing card borders, no chat-GPT-blue buttons, no centered hero metrics. If it looks like a 2024 AI dashboard template, it's wrong.

5. **Motion is functional, not decorative** — Animate to show state change, not to delight. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`. One orchestrated entrance is worth more than scattered micro-interactions.
